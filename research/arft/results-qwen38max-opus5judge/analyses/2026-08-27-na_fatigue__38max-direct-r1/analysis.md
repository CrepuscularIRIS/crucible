## A. Ideation & Planning

**A1.** The hypothesis space was spanned by mechanism *class* but never by mechanism *magnitude*, and the campaign's eventual dead end was already visible in that omission. H1 (slow Na fatigue), H2 (slow K), H3 (Ih) partition the space of what the current *is*, and H4/H5 add null and artifact. But every one of the five bands is stated in units of total spike count on pool protocols, and total spike count is precisely the quantity that later turns out to be near-identical across all candidates: the reference gives long step 25, `slow_na:true` gives 30, `slowK g=1.0` gives 22 (ev22/ev27/ev29). A three-hypothesis design whose discriminating observable spans 22–30 while single-rep seed noise later measures a range of 3 (ev120–124) is a design with roughly one spike of headroom per hypothesis. Fair credit: the agent could not know the noise floor before measuring it, and it did measure it later. But the free simulator was available at zero cost *before* preregistration, and a seed sweep on the reference at ev22 would have cost nothing and revealed that the planned discriminator was marginal. Planning consumed the free oracle for point anchors and never for variance. [stage: A | root cause: depth]

**A2.** Three of the nine pool protocols were never simulated even once, including the two that most directly probe the mechanism the campaign ultimately endorsed. The pool (ev6) contains `"brief hyperpol conditioning + test (-30 uA/40 ms, then +12 uA)"` and `"hyperpol pre-pulse + weak test (-30 uA/250 ms, gap, +6 uA)"` and `"brief step (12 uA, 40 ms)"` — my count over every `world_simulate`/`world_observe` call gives **0, 0, and 0** uses respectively, against 12 for long step and 8 for paired. H10's central claim is a *recovery time constant* of 300–600 ms; the brief-vs-long hyperpolarizing conditioning pair is the textbook way to measure a recovery τ by varying conditioning duration, and it was free to simulate. The agent instead spent its whole grill answering the objection (G17) that "恢复时标是否 ≥300 ms" was untested — an objection it could have closed with a zero-cost simulation at any point. **A3.** The 8-unit budget was allocated as 4 protocols × reps=2, a choice that maximizes protocol breadth and minimizes per-protocol precision, and the campaign's own later analysis shows this was the wrong side of the trade. ## C. Execution & Implementation

**C1 — the aliasing, stated precisely.** The reference paired trace returned at ev23 contains 258 samples spanning a 300 + 60 + 300 = 660 ms protocol, i.e. dt = 2.558 ms. A Hodgkin–Huxley action potential has a full width of roughly 1–2 ms. Nyquist requires dt < ~0.5 ms to represent it; 2.558 ms samples the spike waveform *below once per event*. I re-ran the count independently on the trace preserved in the log: **14 samples exceed 0 mV, and there are exactly 14 upward crossings of 0 mV**, against a meter value of 29 for the same protocol and seed. That is not a threshold-choice disagreement — it is a Shannon-limit disagreement, and no threshold can repair it, because a spike that produces no sample above the level is simply absent from the array. Everything the agent computed from this array — spike times, ISIs, per-pulse decomposition, the f–I inference — inherits a >2× undercount that varies unpredictably with where each spike falls relative to the sampling grid. This is the single defect from which the score follows. [stage: C | root cause: grounding]

**C2 — the threshold was tuned to the answer, not to physiology.** At ev41 the agent swept the threshold and printed, verbatim:

```
thr=0:   total=14 p1=8  gap=0 p2=6
thr=-10: total=19 p1=12 gap=0 p2=7
thr=-20: total=30 p1=16 gap=1 p2=13
thr=-30: total=39 p1=20 gap=3 p2=16
报告值: total=29
```

and adopted −20 mV. The reasoning is explicit in the last line: −20 gives 30, which is nearest to the meter's 29. But −20 mV is not a spike threshold in any physiological sense — it is below the sodium activation midpoint, so subthreshold depolarizations and the shoulders of aliased events all cross it. My independent recomputation confirms the mechanism: on that trace, upward crossings of −20 mV number 30 while *local maxima above −20 mV* also number 30 and samples above 0 mV number 14; the agreement at −20 is coincidence produced by counting depolarization excursions, not spikes. The agent thus reverse-engineered a parameter to match a target, then used the resulting decomposition as independent evidence about the target's structure. Fair credit: matching a known quantity is a legitimate calibration move *if* the calibration is then validated out-of-sample; here it never was. [stage: C | root cause: integrity]

**C3 — the per-pulse decomposition, the artifact's most damaging product.** Because a spike-time list is required to say "how many in pulse 1 versus pulse 2", and because that list is aliased, the split at ev147 (`pulse1 (0-300ms): 8 spikes, gap: 0, pulse2 (360-660ms): 6`) is not just scaled-down but *differentially* wrong: pulse 1 loses 8 of its true count and pulse 2 loses a different number, so the ratio between them — the quantity carrying all the mechanistic content about fatigue — is unconstrained. The agent used exactly this ratio to reason about paired-pulse suppression. A metric that is wrong by an unknown, non-constant factor cannot support a claim about a ratio, yet the ratio is what H10's mechanism story is built from. [stage: C | root cause: grounding]

**C4 — the "non-monotonic f–I" is an artifact of the artifact.** At ev106 the agent concluded that the reference has a non-monotonic frequency–current relation: the 10 uA single pulse gives 25 spikes (meter-authoritative, P2) while the 12 uA first pulse gives only ≈15–16 (agent-derived, aliased). The comparison is between a **meter number** and a **subsample-derived number** — two incommensurable quantities. Correctly compared, both should come from the meter, and the meter never returned a per-pulse figure for the paired protocol; it returned 29 for the whole thing. A monotonic f–I with the meter's own scaling would put the 12 uA first pulse *above* 25, not at 15. So the campaign's central quantitative belief — that more current yields fewer spikes over some range — is a pure measurement artifact with no support whatsoever, and it inverted the sign of the amplitude dependence used in the forecast. [stage: C | root cause: grounding]

**C5 — `evalCommand` was never a real command, and the workaround left preregistration hollow.** P1's spec contains `"evalCommand": "world_observe --world na_fatigue --seed 0 --protocol \"long step (10 uA, 300 ms)\" --reps 2"`, which is an MCP tool descriptor, not a shell invocation; `probe_run` executed it and returned `探针 P1 以退出码 127 结束，不予落地（崩溃、超时与干净结果不可区分，一概拒绝）`. The recovery pattern the agent settled on — preregister bands with a placeholder command, run the observation through the MCP tool, then preregister a *second* probe whose command replays the metric out of `world-ledger.jsonl` — is described in the agent's own ev178 lesson note. This preserves the timestamp ordering that the prereg gate checks, but it changes what `probe_run` means: P2–P5 do not execute measurements, they re-read a ledger the observation already wrote. The report says this plainly ("P2 以同频段重算命令补落地，预登记先于观测由 P1 的冻结时间戳保证"), which is commendable disclosure of a workaround that a less honest agent would have hidden. [stage: C | root cause: integrity]

**C6 — `metric_recompute` is a replay, so the reconcile gate's guarantee is narrower than it appears.** Events 151–154 return `{"pid":"P2","metric":25}`, `{"pid":"P3","metric":14.5}`, `{"pid":"P4","metric":14}`, `{"pid":"P5","metric":26}` — exactly the values `probe_run` landed, which are exactly the values `world_observe` returned. **C7 — the H3 (Ih) refutation rests on a trace that cannot show sag.** The report kills H3 on morphology: `condition 相电压全程钳位 -91 ~ -101 mV、无 ≥5 mV sag`. I recomputed from the ev71 observation (125 samples over 400 ms, dt = 3.2 ms, `test_start_index=68`): the conditioning phase after settling has mean −95.6 mV in its first third and −94.9 mV in its last third, a linear drift of **+1.24 mV over 192 ms**, against a sample-to-sample standard deviation of **2.07 mV**. The claim "全程钳位" is also inaccurate: the trace ranges over [−101.46, −91.20] mV, a 10 mV spread. Fair credit: H3's registered prediction demanded a large, specific signature (`≥5 mV 的向去极化方向 sag 漂移`, "实例：-95 漂向 -80"), and a 15 mV excursion of that kind would indeed have been visible above this noise, so the refutation of *strong* Ih is fair. **C8 — the two data channels have different sampling densities and were treated as one.** `world_observe` returns 100 samples for the 300 ms long step (dt = 3.0 ms), 125 for the 400 ms hyperpol protocol (3.2 ms), 55 for the 120 ms strong step (2.18 ms), and 190 for the 660 ms paired protocol (3.47 ms); `world_simulate` returns 258 for the same 660 ms paired protocol (2.558 ms). The rebuttal answers a question adjacent to the one asked. [stage: C | root cause: grounding]

**C9 — the ISI adaptation analysis compares two traces at different dt and reads the difference as biology.** At ev126 the agent computes adaptation ratios using `dt=3.0` for the observation and `dt=1.2` for the reference, obtaining `obs_long: n_spikes≈8, ratio=2.36` versus `ref_long: n_spikes≈19, ratio=1.39`, and concludes in the report that "观测适应比 ≈2 vs 参考 ≈1，方向支持附加适应电流". But the observation resolves 8 of 25 true spikes and the reference 19 of 25; two series that drop different fractions of their events will show different apparent ISI lengthening for purely sampling reasons, because aliasing preferentially removes closely-spaced early spikes. The `dt=1.2` figure is itself unexplained — the reference long-step array is 250 samples over 300 ms, which is 1.2 ms, so that part is right, but it means the two traces differ 2.5× in resolution. Fair credit: the agent explicitly downgraded this to a hint ("降采样过粗只作线索") and did not transition any claim on it, which is the correct handling of a weak signal. [stage: C | root cause: robustness]

**C10 — the forecast was edited between draft and submission with no recorded justification.** ev148 prints the draft `{paired 10: 45, paired 14: 23, paired 12/350: 29, paired 8: 41, single 12: 15, single 10: 25}`; ev149 submits `{paired 10: 47, paired 12/350: 30, paired 14: 25, paired 8: 42, single 10: 25, single 12: 15}`. Four of six values moved by +1 or +2 in the interval between the two calls, with no intervening computation, simulation, or narration in the log. Small as they are, these edits move the submission *away* from the draft in the direction of higher counts on exactly the protocols that turned out to dominate the error. An unexplained last-moment adjustment to the single scored artifact is the kind of step that should carry a reason. [stage: C | root cause: integrity]

**C11 — the forecast contains two mutually incompatible composition rules.** Take the submitted numbers at face value. `single long 12 uA = 15` together with `paired 12 uA/350+350 = 30` implies the paired response is exactly 2× the single, i.e. no fatigue whatsoever *and* no gain from the longer 350 ms pulses. But `single long 10 uA = 25` together with `paired 10 uA/300+300 = 47` implies a factor of 1.88, i.e. mild fatigue. The same cell cannot obey both. Worse, the 12 uA pair uses *longer* pulses (350 ms) than the 10 uA pair (300 ms) yet is predicted to produce 30 against 47 — a 17-spike deficit for a stronger, longer stimulus. Whatever model generated these six numbers, it was not applied consistently across them. [stage: C | root cause: robustness]

**C12 — the amplitude ordering in the forecast is inverted and monotonic-violating.** Submitted paired counts, sorted by current: 8 uA → 42, 10 uA → 47, 12 uA → 30, 14 uA → 25. Spike count therefore *falls by 22* (−47 %) as drive rises from 10 to 14 uA. For a depolarizing current step in any standard neuron model, that requires depolarization block, and depolarization block at 14 uA is directly contradicted by the reference's own free simulation of the `strong step (18 uA, 120 ms)` protocol, which returns 15 spikes in 120 ms — a rate of 125 Hz, i.e. **higher** than the 10 uA long step's 25 spikes in 300 ms (83 Hz). The agent had this datum from ev38 and it flatly refutes the block story at 14 uA, yet the forecast encodes block anyway. This is a sanity check that costs one division and would have caught the error before submission. [stage: C | root cause: grounding]

**C13 — the only forecast value that was right required no model at all.** `single long 10 uA/300 ms = 25` is a direct copy of the P2 observation. The meter reports 25 spikes for the 10 uA long step and 15 for the 18 uA strong step, and normalizing by duration gives 83 Hz versus 125 Hz — monotonically increasing with current. The "blocking" exists only in the agent's aliased reconstruction. ## X. Cross-Stage Dynamics

**X1 — a single measurement defect propagated through five stages and was strengthened at each.** Trace the chain event by event. At **C/ev23** the API returns `mean_voltage_subsampled` at 2.558 ms and the agent reads it as a voltage trace. At **C/ev41** the threshold is tuned to −20 mV because it reproduces the meter's 29. At **D/ev106** the tuned decomposition yields "the 12 uA first pulse gives only ≈15", which becomes a *non-monotonic f–I* — a striking, mechanistically interesting claim. At **A/ev146** H10 is registered with a prediction clause that encodes it (`低幅值成对（8 uA）累积少→相对更接近参考外推`). At **C/ev149** the forecast submits 42 and 47 for the two low-amplitude paired protocols. At **E/§4** the failure is attributed to over-generalizing the reference's amplitude scaling — which preserves the artifact by treating the non-monotonicity as a real property of the reference. At no point does the belief weaken; each stage re-encodes it in a more authoritative form, from a threshold parameter to a registered claim to a scored submission to a published post-mortem. This is the canonical shape of error propagation through a well-run pipeline: rigor at every stage after the defect amplifies rather than filters it, because every stage is checking *consistency with the previous stage*. [stage: X | root cause: grounding]

**X2 — the campaign is right about the science and wrong about the number, and the two are not independent.** §1's qualitative conclusion — no strong deviation from the reference detected within 8 units of budget, survivors reduce to a disjunction, the null model is not excluded — is well-supported by my own recomputation of the residuals (0, +0.5, −1, −3, all inside ±4). Meanwhile the scored deliverable is off by RMSE 13.4. These look like separate outcomes but share one cause: the same aliasing that produced the fake f–I also destroyed the per-pulse and ISI structure that was the *only* channel through which a weak fatigue mechanism could have been distinguished from the null. Had the traces been read correctly, the campaign might have had discriminating power on the very question it concluded was underdetermined. The honest null result is therefore partly an artifact of the same defect that produced the dishonest forecast — the agent is right for reasons that are partly wrong. [stage: X | root cause: grounding]

**X3 — process rigor was inversely correlated with measurement contact, and the incentive gradient explains it.** Every one of the campaign's rigorous behaviors is cheap and verifiable: registering claims, freezing bands, recording attacks, passing gates, reproducing conclusion lines verbatim. Every behavior that would have caught the actual error is neither: sanity-checking a trace against the meter, sweeping seeds on all four anchors, observing an amplitude ladder, spending reps instead of protocols. The apparatus rewards the first set with visible green checkmarks and the second set with nothing. Over 179 events the agent optimized toward the gradient the environment provided — roughly 30 world-touching calls against 100+ ledger and process operations — and the environment's gradient did not point at the data. This is a property of the harness as much as of the agent, and it is the most transferable finding in this trajectory: a verification architecture that checks provenance, ordering, and citation will certify a campaign whose central measurement is off by 2×. [stage: X | root cause: robustness]

**X4 — goal drift from "identify the mechanism" to "complete the pipeline".** The stated campaign question is `真细胞在参考模型之外携带何种未知附加膜电流？` — which extra current does the cell carry? The delivered answer is a disjunction over three claims, one of which is the null model, with the report itself conceding that the evidence establishes only `H4 ∨ (弱机制)`. Meanwhile the RULINGS entry defines success in terms of pipeline completion (`任务要求完整管线…证据链需达赛事级`) and `research-loop/SKILL.md` defines done as `战役完成 = mcp__research__report_declare 收到三道 gate 裁决事件`. By the second definition the campaign succeeded completely; by the first it returned "we don't know" at a cost of 8 observations and an MSE of 179. When the operational definition of done is a gate verdict rather than an answer, the two can diverge without anything in the system noticing — and here they did. [stage: X | root cause: depth]

**Nyquist check.** dt = 2.558 ms against an HH spike full-width of ~1–2 ms: the sampling interval exceeds the event duration, so a spike may contribute one sample, or none, depending on phase. Undercounting is guaranteed and its magnitude is phase-dependent, hence non-constant across pulses — which is why the per-pulse *ratio* is unrecoverable.
